Preliminary
R.1Short title & commencement
Called the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. Came into force on 1 April 2011.
R.2Definitions
  • Dealer — anyone buying/selling/supplying a packaged commodity (excludes a manufacturer selling only to dealers).
  • Lot — total packages stored, or the maximum hourly output on a packing line.
  • Manufacturer — who makes/produces the commodity, or whose brand mark claims it as theirs.
  • Maximum permissible error — allowed deficiency in a package's quantity, per the First Schedule.
  • Net quantity — quantity of commodity in a package, excluding packaging/wrappers.
  • Packer — who pre-packs a commodity into sale-ready units.
  • Principal display panel — the surface of a package where required declarations appear.
  • Retail dealer — sells directly to the end consumer.
  • Retail package — meant for sale to the ultimate consumer (excludes industrial/institutional buyers).
  • Retail sale price (MRP) — the maximum price at which the package may be sold, printed inclusive of all taxes.
  • Wholesale dealer — sells only through intermediaries, not directly to consumers.
  • Wholesale package — a package of multiple retail packages, or bulk sold to an intermediary, or a pack of 10+ retail packages.
Chapter II — Provisions applicable to packages for retail sale
R.3Applicability of the Chapter
Does NOT apply to: packages >25 kg/25 litre (except cement & fertiliser bags up to 50 kg), and commodities meant for industrial or institutional consumers (airlines, hotels, hospitals, etc. buying direct from the manufacturer for their own use).
R.4Regulation for pre-packing & sale
No one may pre-pack a commodity for sale unless the package bears (or has a label with) all declarations required under these rules. Packages inside the manufacturer's own premises need not yet show MRP — but must before leaving for their destination.
R.5Standard package sizes
Commodities listed in the Second Schedule (e.g. baby food, biscuits, edible oil, salt, cement, soft drinks) must be packed in the specified standard quantities. If packed in a different size, the label must say "Not a standard pack size" / "non-standard size" under these Rules.
R.6Declarations on every package
Every retail package must declare:
  • Name & address of the manufacturer/packer (and importer, if imported)
  • Common/generic name of the commodity
  • Net quantity (standard unit of weight/measure/number)
  • Month & year of manufacture/pre-packing/import
  • Retail sale price (MRP), inclusive of all taxes
  • Dimensions of the commodity, if relevant
  • Consumer-care contact: name, address, phone, email of the complaints contact
Exceptions: bidi/incense sticks and PSU domestic LPG cylinders need no month/year; bidis and price-controlled LPG cylinders need no MRP. Food articles follow the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act instead. Stickers cannot cover a declaration, except to lower MRP.
R.7Principal display panel — size & letter height
Sets minimum numeral/letter heights based on net quantity or panel area (from 1 mm up to 6 mm for larger packages/areas). Letters must be at least 1 mm tall (2 mm if moulded/embossed), with width ≥ ⅓ of height.

Up to 200 g/ml 1 mm
200–500 g/ml 2 mm

R.8Where the declaration must appear
All declarations must be on the principal display panel, with clear space around the quantity figure. For returnable soft-drink bottles, MRP may instead be on the crown cap.
R.9Manner of declaration
Declarations must be legible, prominent, and (for price/quantity) in a colour contrasting with the background. Language: Hindi (Devnagri) or English (other languages may be added too). Outer wrappers must repeat all declarations unless transparent.
R.10Name & address of manufacturer
Every package must conspicuously show the manufacturer's (or packer's/importer's) complete postal address — enough for a consumer to locate them (city, state, and PIN code at minimum). Must be the actual registered/corporate name.
R.11General rules for declaring quantity
Net quantity excludes wrapper weight. If quantity doesn't vary with environment, no "when packed" qualifier is allowed. Commodities that do vary significantly (Third Schedule — soaps, lotions, creams) may use "when packed."
R.12Manner of declaring quantity
Unit must match commodity type: mass (solids), length (linear-sold goods), area, volume (liquids), or number. "Dozen," "score," "gross" etc. may never be used as the unit.
R.13Units of weight/measure/number
Below 1 kg/1 m/1 litre → use grams/centimetres/millilitres. At or above → kilogram/metre/litre, with fractions in decimals. Only SI units allowed; number-sold items use symbol "N" or "U."
R.14Dimensions of certain commodities
Bed-sheets, dhoties, sarees, towels, table-cloths etc. must declare the number of pieces and finished dimensions; if pieces differ in size, each size and its price must be shown.
R.15Dimensions & weight tied to price
If a commodity's price depends on its size/weight, that dimension or weight must also be declared alongside the quantity.
R.16Number of usable sheets
Foil, tissues, toilet paper and similar sheet products must state the number of usable sheets and the dimensions of each.
R.17Container-type commodities
Bags, boxes, cups, pans etc. sold as containers must declare number + linear dimensions (or diameter/depth for round containers).
R.18Provisions for wholesale & retail dealers
Dealers may only sell compliant packages, never above MRP. If tax changes after packing, the revised MRP must be publicised (2 newspaper ads + notices to dealers & Legal Metrology authorities) and the increase can't exceed the actual tax hike. VAT-registered retailers selling by weight/volume must keep a class-III accuracy electronic weighing scale with printed receipts for consumers to verify.
R.19Inspection at manufacturer/packer's premises
A Director/Controller/Legal Metrology Officer may inspect and sample packages per the Fifth Schedule (32 samples if lot <4000, 80 if more), test per the Sixth Schedule, and record results (Seventh Schedule Form A/B). Fresh tests cost ₹2,500. A lot passes only if the average quantity ≥ declared, no package exceeds max permissible error, and all declarations are present.
R.20Action after inspection
If a package fails the test, the officer seizes samples as evidence and initiates action — unless a valid retest (Rule 19(4)) was already carried out.
R.21Inspection at dealer's premises
Testing here only happens if: a complaint is received, tampering/pilferage is suspected, or declarations seem missing. Packages marked "when packed" are protected if any deficiency is due to genuine environmental conditions.
R.22Establishing maximum permissible error
Permissible error accounts for unavoidable weighing/counting deviation, normal transport/storage/climate exposure, and packaging-material variation. Specified in the First Schedule.
R.23Deceptive packages
A package that's oversized purely to mislead the consumer about quantity ("deceptive package") must be repacked/relabelled by the manufacturer, or else seized — unless the larger size is genuinely needed to protect the commodity or fit filling-machine requirements.
Chapter III — Wholesale packages
R.24Declarations on wholesale packages
Every wholesale package must legibly declare: name/address of manufacturer or importer (or packer), the commodity's identity, and either the number of retail packages inside or the total net quantity.
Chapter IV — Export of packaged commodities
R.25Restrictions on selling export packages in India
A package made for export cannot be sold in India unless it's re-packed/re-labelled to comply with Chapter II — otherwise it's liable to seizure.
Chapter V — Exemptions
R.26Exempted packages
These rules don't apply to:
  • Packages of 10 g/10 ml or less (though 10–20 g/ml still needs MRP + net quantity)
  • Fast-food packed by a restaurant/hotel
  • Drug formulations under the Drugs (Price Control) Order, 1995
  • Agricultural produce packed above 50 kg
Chapter VI — Registration of manufacturers, packers & importers
R.27Registration requirement
Anyone pre-packing or importing a commodity must register (name + complete address) with the Director/Controller, paying a ₹500 fee, within 90 days of starting such business. Amending a registration certificate costs ₹100. Incomplete applications are returned within 7 working days.
R.28Shorter address registration
Manufacturers/packers may separately register a shorter address, if the Director/Controller is satisfied it's still sufficient for consumer identification.
R.29Public register
The Director/Controller maintains a register of all registered manufacturers/packers, open to public inspection free of charge.
R.30Compilation & circulation of lists
A state-wise list of registered manufacturers/packers is compiled and circulated to State Controllers, to enable sampling at their premises.
Chapter VII — General & penalties
R.31Advertisements
Any ad quoting the MRP must also state net quantity/number, in the same font size as the price.
R.32Penalty for contravention
Breach of Rules 27–31 (registration/advertising): fine of ₹4,000. Breach of any other rule with no specified punishment: fine of ₹2,000.
R.33Power to relax
The Central Government may relax specific provisions for a reasonable period, after verifying a genuine compounding/court decision, with suitable corrective measures.
R.34Repeal & savings
Repeals the Standards of Weights and Measures (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 1977 — but past actions, rights, penalties, registrations and pending proceedings under the old rules remain valid/continue under these new Rules.
Schedules
Sch. IMaximum permissible errors
Tables of allowed deficiency by declared quantity — e.g. up to 50 g/ml: 9%; 100–200 g: 4.5%; 1000–10000 g: 1.5%; above 15000 g: 1.0%. Separate table for length/area/number (2–4%).
Sch. IICommodities with mandated standard pack sizes
Lists fixed pack sizes for baby food, weaning food, biscuits, bread, butter/margarine, cereals & pulses, coffee, tea, milk powder, detergents, atta/rice/rawa, salt, soaps, aerated/soft drinks, mineral water, cement, and paints/varnish.
Sch. IIICommodities allowed "when packed" qualifier
All soaps, lotions, and creams (other than cream of milk) — since their weight can vary after packing.
Sch. IVExceptions to the standard unit rule
Commodities that may be declared by weight OR another unit instead of the default: aerosols, curd, electric cable/wire (length or weight), fencing wire (number or weight), fruits, LPG (weight), nails/screws (number or weight), paints, ready-made garments (number), tyres (number), yarn (weight or length), cosmetics (weight or measure).
Sch. VSample selection manner
Sample size: 32 packages for a lot under 4,000; 80 for a lot of 4,000 or more. Samples are drawn at random from various points of the stock (top, bottom, centre, sides, front, rear).
Sch. VIMethod for determining net quantity
Details the equipment, working standards, and step-by-step tare/gross weighing procedure (also for liquids by volume, and length/number counts) used to verify a package's actual contents.
Sch. VIIData-sheet forms (A & B)
Form A — Weight-checking data sheet. Form B — Volume/length-checking data sheet. Both record package particulars, sample results, and signatures of the manufacturer/packer and inspecting officer.
